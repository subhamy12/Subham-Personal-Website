UK: Lowering the Voting Age to 16

The UK government plans to let 16- and 17-year-olds vote in general elections, as they already can in Scotland and Wales for devolved elections.

Reading level

Right now you have to be 18 to vote in UK general elections. The government wants to lower it to 16. Teens in Scotland and Wales can already vote at 16 in their local and parliament elections. The plan also includes changes like more accepted voter ID and tighter rules on political donations.

What changes

  • Voting age for UK general elections lowered to 16
  • Wider voter ID options
  • Steps toward automatic registration
  • Tighter donation rules

Who’s affected

positive16–17 year oldsGain the vote in UK-wide elections.
mixedPolitical partiesYoung voters have tended to lean toward Labour, Greens and Lib Dems.

Each side is written the way its own supporters would make the case. Neither is endorsed. The middle column covers what is actually contested.

Case from the left

If you can work and pay tax at 16, you should get a say.

  • Matches existing rules in Scotland and Wales.
  • Builds lifelong voting habits.
Neutral read

Evidence from Scotland suggests 16–17 year olds who get the vote turn out at similar or higher rates than 18–24 year olds. The partisan effect on national results is likely small given the number of voters involved.

Open questions
  • When will legislation be introduced?
  • Will it apply before the next general election?
Case from the right

Changes the rules in a way that may help the governing party.

  • Most legal adult rights start at 18.
  • Critics say it was not in the manifesto debate in detail.
