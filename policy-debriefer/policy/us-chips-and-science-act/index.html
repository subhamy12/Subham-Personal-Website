CHIPS and Science Act (2022)

A US law that pays companies to build computer-chip factories in America and boosts funding for science research.

Reading level

Almost all advanced computer chips are made in Asia. This law gives chip companies money and tax breaks to build factories in the US, so the country depends less on other places. It also sets higher targets for spending on science research. Companies that take the money can't expand advanced chipmaking in China.

What changes

  • About $39B in grants for building chip factories in the US
  • 25% tax credit for investment in chip manufacturing
  • Higher authorised budgets for science research agencies
  • Funding recipients restricted from expanding advanced chipmaking in China

Who’s affected

positiveChip manufacturersGrants and tax credits for new US factories.

Each side is written the way its own supporters would make the case. Neither is endorsed. The middle column covers what is actually contested.

Case from the left

Smart industrial policy that brings good jobs home.

  • Rebuilds US manufacturing and creates union construction jobs.
  • Some progressives objected to large subsidies for profitable companies.
Neutral read

Support and opposition crossed party lines: both parties' leaders backed it on security grounds, while critics on both sides questioned subsidies to large firms. Results depend on whether factories are finished on time and on budget.

Open questions
  • Will the funded factories reach full production?
  • Will Congress fund the authorised science budgets?
Case from the right

A national-security investment, though some call it corporate welfare.

  • Reduces dependence on chips made near China.
  • Fiscal conservatives argued the government shouldn't pick winning industries.
