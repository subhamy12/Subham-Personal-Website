Mexico: Electing Judges by Popular Vote

Mexico changed its constitution so that judges, including Supreme Court justices, are chosen by voters in elections instead of being appointed.

Reading level

In most countries judges are appointed. Mexico now has voters elect them, including the top Supreme Court judges. The Supreme Court was also made smaller. The first judicial election was held in June 2025, and only a small share of voters took part.

What changes

  • Judges and Supreme Court justices chosen by popular vote
  • Supreme Court cut from 11 to 9 justices
  • New tribunal to discipline judges
  • Candidates nominated by the executive, legislature and judiciary

Who’s affected

negativeSitting judgesMany had to stand for election or leave.

Each side is written the way its own supporters would make the case. Neither is endorsed. The middle column covers what is actually contested.

Case from the left

Ends an unaccountable judicial elite and fights corruption.

  • Gives citizens a direct say over a powerful branch.
  • Supporters say courts protected the wealthy and blocked reforms.
Neutral read

Mexico is one of very few countries to elect its entire judiciary. The main debate is whether elections increase accountability or make judges more political. Early evidence is limited to a single, low-turnout election.

Open questions
  • Will elected judges rule against the government?
  • Will turnout rise in later judicial elections?
Case from the right

Weakens judicial independence and concentrates power.

  • Candidates nominated by politicians may owe them loyalty.
  • Low turnout undermines the claim of a popular mandate.
