India: Digital Personal Data Protection Act (2023)

India's first broad privacy law: companies need your consent to use your personal data and must protect it, with large fines, but the government keeps wide exemptions.

Reading level

This law says apps and companies need your permission before using your personal data, must tell you why, and must let you see, correct or delete it. A new Data Protection Board can fine them heavily if they leak data. Critics say the government gave itself broad powers to skip these rules.

What changes

  • Consent required to process personal data
  • Rights to access, correct and erase your data
  • Fines up to ₹250 crore per breach
  • Broad exemptions available to government agencies

Who’s affected

positiveInternet usersNew rights over their personal data.
mixedBusinessesCompliance costs, but clearer rules than before.
negativeJournalists and RTI usersCritics say changes make it harder to get public officials' information.

Each side is written the way its own supporters would make the case. Neither is endorsed. The middle column covers what is actually contested.

Case from the left

A privacy law with too many loopholes for the state.

  • Government exemptions could enable surveillance.
  • The Data Protection Board isn't independent enough.
Neutral read

Most observers welcome a baseline privacy law; the dispute is about state exemptions and the Board's independence. Its real effect depends on how strictly the Board enforces the rules.

Open questions
  • How independent will the Data Protection Board be in practice?
Case from the right

A practical privacy law that doesn't strangle innovation.

  • Simpler than Europe's GDPR, easier for startups.
  • Security exemptions are needed to protect the country.
