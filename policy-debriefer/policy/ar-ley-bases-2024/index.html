Argentina: Ley Bases (Milei Reform Package)

A large Argentine law that lets the president reorganise government for a year, opens some state companies to privatisation, and offers big incentives for large investments.

Reading level

President Javier Milei promised to shrink the state and fix the economy. This law, cut down from a much bigger first version, gives him temporary extra powers to reorganise government agencies, allows some state-owned companies to be sold, changes some labour rules and offers tax and customs perks to companies that invest at least $200 million.

What changes

  • Temporary delegated powers for the president
  • Several state companies can be privatised
  • Labour rule changes
  • Incentive regime (RIGI) for investments over US$200M

Who’s affected

positiveLarge investors (mining, energy)Long-term tax and currency stability guarantees.
negativePublic-sector workersAgency restructuring and possible job cuts.
negativeHigher earnersIncome tax reinstated by the companion fiscal law.

Each side is written the way its own supporters would make the case. Neither is endorsed. The middle column covers what is actually contested.

Case from the left

Hands power to the president and sells off public assets.

  • Delegated powers bypass Congress.
  • Investment perks favour foreign corporations over local workers.
Neutral read

The law passed only after major concessions, showing the limits of the government's congressional support. Its economic effect is hard to separate from the wider stabilisation program and inflation trends.

Open questions
  • How many large projects will use RIGI?
  • Which privatisations will actually happen?
Case from the right

Essential reforms to end decades of stagnation and inflation.

  • A smaller state frees the private economy.
  • RIGI attracts investment Argentina badly needs.
