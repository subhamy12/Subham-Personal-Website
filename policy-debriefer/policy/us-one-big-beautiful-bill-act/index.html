One Big Beautiful Bill Act (2025)

A large US budget law that makes the 2017 income tax cuts permanent, adds new deductions like tips and overtime, and pays for part of it with Medicaid and food-aid changes.

Reading level

This law keeps the lower income tax rates from 2017 from expiring, and for a few years lets many workers deduct tips and overtime pay. It adds money for border and immigration enforcement and the military. To offset costs, it adds work rules for some adults on Medicaid and SNAP (food aid) and winds down several clean-energy tax credits sooner. Independent scorekeepers project it adds to the national debt.

What changes

  • Makes 2017 individual tax rate cuts permanent
  • Temporary deductions for tip and overtime income
  • Work requirements for many working-age adults on Medicaid
  • SNAP eligibility and cost-sharing changes for states
  • More funding for border enforcement and defense; earlier end to some clean-energy credits

Each side is written the way its own supporters would make the case. Neither is endorsed. The middle column covers what is actually contested.

Case from the left

Tax cuts tilted to the wealthy, paid for by cutting health care and food aid.

  • Millions projected to lose health coverage.
  • Adds trillions to the debt while top earners gain the most.
  • Rolls back clean-energy investment and jobs.
Neutral read

Whether this is a 'tax cut' or 'tax extension' depends on the baseline: compared with expiring law it lowers taxes; compared with 2025 policy it mostly keeps them the same. Coverage effects depend on how states implement work verification.

Open questions
  • How will states verify work hours?
  • Will the temporary deductions be extended past 2028?
Case from the right

Prevents a huge tax hike and rewards work.

  • Stops a tax increase that would have hit most families in 2026.
  • No tax on tips and overtime helps working people directly.
  • Work requirements return programs to their original purpose and reduce fraud.
