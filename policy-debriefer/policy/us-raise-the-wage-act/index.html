US Raise the Wage Act ($17 Federal Minimum)

A proposal to gradually raise the US federal minimum wage from $7.25 an hour to $17, and phase out the lower wage for tipped workers.

Reading level

The lowest legal hourly pay in the US has been $7.25 since 2009, though many states set it higher. This bill would raise it step by step to $17 and then link it to typical wage growth. Workers who earn tips would eventually get the full minimum wage from their employer too. It hasn't passed.

What changes

  • Phased increase to $17/hour
  • Automatic future increases tied to wage growth
  • End to subminimum wages for tipped, youth and disabled workers

Who’s affected

positiveLow-wage workersHigher pay, especially in states at the $7.25 floor.
negativeSmall businessesHigher labour costs, particularly in lower-cost regions.
unclearSome entry-level jobsEconomists disagree on the size of job losses.

Each side is written the way its own supporters would make the case. Neither is endorsed. The middle column covers what is actually contested.

Case from the left

$7.25 is a poverty wage; workers deserve a raise.

  • Minimum wage has lost value to inflation since 2009.
  • Reduces reliance on public assistance.
Neutral read

Research on modest increases often finds small employment effects; large increases in low-cost areas are less studied. About 30 states already set a higher minimum.

Open questions
  • How large would job losses be in low-wage regions?
Case from the right

A one-size-fits-all mandate that costs jobs.

  • Living costs vary hugely between states.
  • Small businesses may cut hours or automate.
