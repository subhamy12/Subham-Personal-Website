EU Artificial Intelligence Act

The EU's rulebook for AI: the riskier an AI system is, the stricter the rules — and a few uses, like government social scoring, are banned outright.

Reading level

The EU sorts AI into risk levels. Some uses are banned (like ranking citizens by behaviour). 'High-risk' AI — used in hiring, schools, loans or policing — has to be tested, documented and overseen by people. Chatbots and deepfakes must tell you they're AI. Big general-purpose AI models must share info about how they were trained. Rules phase in from 2025 to 2027.

What changes

  • Bans some uses, such as social scoring and certain manipulative AI
  • Testing, documentation and human oversight for high-risk AI
  • Disclosure duties for chatbots and AI-generated media
  • Transparency and safety duties for general-purpose AI models
  • Fines linked to a company's worldwide turnover

Who’s affected

positiveEU residentsMore transparency and protections when AI makes decisions about them.

Each side is written the way its own supporters would make the case. Neither is endorsed. The middle column covers what is actually contested.

Case from the left

A needed guardrail that puts people's rights first.

  • Protects against biased or opaque automated decisions.
  • Some advocates say the policing exemptions are too broad.
Neutral read

Support and criticism don't map neatly onto left/right: business groups across the spectrum worry about competitiveness, while civil-liberties groups across the spectrum push for stronger limits on surveillance.

Open questions
  • How strictly will national authorities enforce it?
  • Will timelines be delayed or simplified?
Case from the right

Heavy regulation that risks pushing innovation out of Europe.

  • Compliance burden could slow European AI startups.
  • Rules may become outdated as technology moves fast.
