Germany: Debt Brake Reform for Defense & Infrastructure

Germany changed its constitution so it can borrow much more for defense and set up a €500 billion fund for roads, rail and climate projects.

Germany has a rule in its constitution limiting how much the government can borrow. In 2025, parliament loosened it: defense spending above 1% of GDP no longer counts toward the limit, and a €500 billion special fund was created for infrastructure, with part going to climate projects.

What changes

  • Defense spending above 1% of GDP exempt from borrowing limit
  • €500bn infrastructure fund
  • Some extra borrowing room for states

Who’s affected

positiveArmed forces & defense industryMuch larger long-term budgets.
negativeFuture taxpayersHigher public debt and interest costs.
positiveInfrastructure usersFunding for rail, roads, digital networks.

Each side is written the way its own supporters would make the case. Neither is endorsed. The middle column covers what is actually contested.

Case from the left

Overdue investment — though some on the left oppose the defense build-up.

  • Germany's infrastructure has been underfunded for years.
  • Climate earmark was a Green condition.
Neutral read

This was a centrist cross-party deal; opposition came from both the far left and far right, for different reasons.

Open questions
  • Can money be spent fast enough given planning bottlenecks?
Case from the right

Necessary for security, but a break from fiscal discipline.

  • Russia's war requires stronger defense.
  • Fiscal hawks warn about long-term debt.
