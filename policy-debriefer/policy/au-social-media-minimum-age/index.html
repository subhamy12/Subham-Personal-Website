Australia: Social Media Minimum Age (Under-16 Ban)

Australia requires major social media platforms to keep under-16s from having accounts, with big fines for platforms that don't take reasonable steps.

Reading level

Kids under 16 in Australia aren't allowed to have accounts on major social media apps. It's the companies — not parents or kids — that get fined if they don't make a real effort to stop it. Messaging, gaming and some education and health apps are left out.

What changes

  • Under-16s cannot hold accounts on designated social media platforms
  • Platforms face fines up to A$49.5M for not taking reasonable steps
  • Government ID can't be the only age check offered
  • Some messaging, gaming, health and education services exempt

Case from the left

Protects kids from harmful algorithms — but watch the privacy trade-offs.

  • Holds tech companies responsible, not families.
  • Some youth advocates warn it cuts off marginalised teens from support networks.
Neutral read

This passed with cross-party support, so the main debate is not left vs right but child safety vs privacy and practicality. Effectiveness depends on how well age-assurance technology works.

Open questions
  • How accurate is age estimation technology?
  • Will teens move to less regulated platforms?
Case from the right

Backs parents against Big Tech — though some see government overreach.

  • Supports parents who struggle to enforce limits alone.
  • Libertarian critics say parents, not the state, should decide.
